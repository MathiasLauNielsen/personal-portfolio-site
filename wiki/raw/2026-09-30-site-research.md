# Sharpen proof and prices to win enquiries

The MLN Data Consulting site already has the structure that the stronger solo consultancies use. It has problem-framed offer pages, a three-rung way to buy (hours plus two fixed-scope products), labelled result figures and a short form. What it lacks are the three things that most clearly separate the best solo sites from the rest:

- a visible price or price floor on the entry offers;
- proof attached to each offer: anonymised case cards, a buyer testimonial and a real photo;
- a response promise that is actually kept.

This matters because the site's main job is verification, not discovery. **80% of buyers check a referred provider's website, and 51.9% have ruled out a referred firm without ever talking to it.** The top reason, cited by 43.6%, is not being able to tell what the firm does ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/referral-marketing-and-your-professional-services-brand)). Once someone does enquire, replying within an hour makes a lead **about 7× more likely to qualify** than replying an hour later ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)).

About ten fixes are pure code and copy and can ship this week. They include:

- showing context the site already has written but does not display;
- giving each page its own link preview;
- removing the empty blog link;
- making the privacy policy match the form;
- using the same offer names on every page;
- putting proof and product cards on the offer pages.

The higher-value changes need Mathias: prices, a photo, buyer testimonials, confirmed durations and availability, and switching on lead emails.

The evidence has limits. There is no controlled study of solo technical consultants, and the pricing and call-to-action (CTA) evidence comes from surveys of software buyers, expert opinion and vendor data. Treat each change as a bet and measure it with the site's existing `enquiry_sent` tracking.

The Danish market has a gap worth taking. Web search found no Danish solo data engineer with a selling website. The visible Danish sellers of AI coding team setup publish either no prices or no proof.

Evidence strength is labelled throughout:

- **Strong:** a measured audit or a large behavioural benchmark.
- **Moderate:** a large buyer survey, but not specific to consultants.
- **Weak:** expert opinion, vendor data, or patterns seen on peer sites without conversion data.

## Half of referred buyers rule a firm out before calling

For a new solo consultant, most visitors will not arrive cold. Across the surveys found, personal network and referrals are the main source of work for independent consultants. In one survey, 63% of consultants use networking and referrals, and for over half of them 60% of business comes via referral. The same source reports that **70% of consultants get zero leads a month from their website** and 88% do not show fees ([Consulting Success](https://www.consultingsuccess.com/consulting-statistics)). Its sample and method are not disclosed. Danish freelancers look the same: 50% find most clients through their network, though that survey had only 136 respondents, mostly marketing freelancers ([Factofly](https://www.factofly.com/en/blog/freelancing-in-denmark-2024/)). Brokers such as 7N, emagine, Worksome and Right People Group are a real Danish channel. Public buyers mostly reach IT consultants through SKI framework agreements, where brokers are the suppliers ([Right People Group SKI page](https://rightpeoplegroup.com/da/ski-rammeaftale)).

The site therefore mainly serves four groups:

- people who were referred;
- people clicking through from LinkedIn;
- broker account managers vetting him;
- a slower long tail from search and AI answer engines.

The buyer-behaviour data explains what those visitors need. They research alone and prefer not to talk to a seller. Gartner found 61–75% of B2B buyers prefer a rep-free experience across three surveys from 2022 to 2026 ([Gartner 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience); [Gartner 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-09-gartner-sales-survey-finds-67-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-experience)). A 2024 survey found 78% consume three or more pieces of content before contacting a seller. Content that "uses data/research to support claims" (51%) is the top trigger for a sales call ([Demand Gen Report 2024](https://53a3b3d3789413ab876e-c1e3bb10b0333d7ff7aa972d61f8c669.ssl.cf1.rackcdn.com/DGR_DG283_SURV_ContentPref_April_2024_Final.pdf)). These are moderate-strength surveys of B2B buyers in general.

The Hinge referral figures are the most directly relevant, but they date from 2015. Their direction matches everything newer: a referred buyer who cannot quickly see what you do, for whom, and with what result, leaves silently.

Three measured findings shape the mechanics:

- **Response speed.** The HBR audit of 2,241 US companies found that replying within an hour made a lead nearly **7× more likely to qualify** than replying an hour later, and **over 60× more likely** than waiting 24 hours or more ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)). Strong evidence, although dated and from sales teams.
- **Mobile.** In professional services, **81% of landing-page visits are mobile**, and mobile converts about 40% worse than desktop ([Unbounce 2024](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/professional-services-conversion-rate/)).
- **Plain writing.** In the same benchmark, pages written at a 7th-grade reading level or simpler (roughly age 12–13) converted at 12.9%, against 6.6% one band up. Pages of about 275–745 words performed best ([Unbounce 2024](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/professional-services-conversion-rate/)).

These three points favour what MLN already does (short plain copy, a short form, a sticky mobile bar), provided the reply promise is kept.

## Stronger solo peers publish a priced first step and one-metric proof

Fourteen data consultancies were checked on their own sites, eight of them clearly solo. The strongest position on **a problem and outcome for a named buyer**, with the tech stack as supporting detail. Two examples:

- Joe Leavitt: "I fix and build data tech stacks so your reporting is easy, automated, accurate" ([thejestr](https://www.thejestr.com/)).
- Kahan Data Solutions: "Clean up the data behind your AI and reporting projects" ([Kahan](https://www.kahandatasolutions.com/)).

Several name the alternative the buyer is weighing:

- Hikmah compares itself with "the $150–200k senior hire you'd spend six months landing" ([Hikmah](https://hikmahtechnologies.com/)).
- A14K opens with "Most data contractors aren't set up for SMEs. We are." ([A14K](https://www.a14k.co.uk/)).

"AI-ready data" has become the common 2026 framing. It sells data platform work as the precondition for AI, which maps directly onto MLN's two offers. The research on specialisation leans the same way, but it is correlational:

- Hinge finds the fastest-growing firms are **3× more likely to be highly specialised** ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/growth-strategy-for-professional-services-firms)).
- Philip Morgan argues narrow positioning matters most exactly when strangers must find and trust you ([Pemberley summary of Morgan](https://trypemberley.com/blog/specialize-or-stay-a-generalist)).
- Tom Critchlow's counterpoint is that generalists can win through relationships and a clear point of view ([Critchlow](https://tomcritchlow.com/2022/06/16/rejecting-specialization/)).

Specialising by problem rather than industry fits the site's industry-agnostic rule.

A minority of solo sites publish prices, but those that do are the most concrete sellers. Their offers follow the same **three-rung ladder**:

1. a small fixed-scope diagnostic;
2. a scoped build;
3. an ongoing fractional retainer.

USD amounts below are converted at an assumed $1 ≈ €0.86. The notes give no dated rate, so recheck before quoting.

The most distinctive device is **risk reversal on the entry offer**. Examples: Hikmah's audit fee is credited toward a build; Boring Data offers a 7-day money-back guarantee; Joe Leavitt sends "no invoice charged if no work occurs in a given week" ([thejestr](https://www.thejestr.com/services/ongoing-support)).

The price-transparency evidence is **weak to moderate**. TrustRadius finds 81% of buyers want to find pricing on their own, and transparent pricing has been buyers' top wish every year it asked. Those are software buyers, not services buyers ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/b2b-tech-buyers-crave-pricing-transparency)). Experts split:

- **For publishing:** Stark defines a productized service as sold "at a specific, published price" ([Stark](https://jonathanstark.com/daily/20180124-productized-consulting)).
- **Cautious:** David C. Baker would publish only for packaged services and talk about money early ([Baker](https://punctuation.com/publish-your-pricing/)).

No A/B test exists for consulting.

Proof on the stronger solo sites takes four forms:

- **Anonymised one-metric case cards.** Joe Leavitt uses cards labelled by industry and stack, with pseudonymous clients and one buyer-facing metric each, such as "4 hrs saved per week on data calls" and "10x faster client turnaround, days to hours" ([thejestr case studies](https://www.thejestr.com/case-studies)). This pattern suits MLN's confidentiality constraints almost exactly.
- **Named testimonials with title and company.** Examples are Kevin Leary's TripAdvisor VP of Engineering ([kevinleary.net](https://www.kevinleary.net/services/bigquery-consultant/)) and Unytics's quote from Nickel's President ([Unytics](https://unytics.io/)).
- **Counts plus a founder photo.** Kahan shows "30+ data teams" and "11 yrs in data engineering" next to his photo ([Kahan](https://www.kahandatasolutions.com/)).
- **Audience or open-source authorship** in place of logos, as with SQLFluff at A14K and BigFunctions at Unytics.

Nielsen Norman Group's eye-tracking found users looked at real portrait photos on bio pages longer than they read the bio, while generic stock photos were ignored ([secondary summary of NN/g](https://marketleaderbiz.com/nielsen-norman-5-7-seconds-to-engage-in-2026/)). That is the best available support for a photo, though the primary article was not verified.

Calls to action follow a clear pattern. The dominant primary CTA is a free 15–30 minute call booked in a calendar tool. The better sites give that call a name and an output, such as Stratalytic's "Data Impact Scan", "no-obligation, 30 minutes" ([Stratalytic](https://stratalytic.nl/en)), or Unytics's free "Strategy Audit" ([Unytics](https://unytics.io/)). The secondary CTA is nearly always a self-check for visitors not ready to talk: Hikmah's 18-check "Platform Teardown" or Kahan's scoring tool. Honest capacity statements also recur, doubling as scarcity:

- Hikmah takes "two audits monthly; one full-time engagement at a time" ([Hikmah pricing post](https://hikmahtechnologies.com/blog/what-data-engineering-costs-and-how-i-price/)).
- DB DATA publishes its next start date ([DB DATA](https://dbdata.nl/)).

The evidence on booking is weak and vendor-sourced. Chili Piper claims customers go from ≈50% to ≈80% of inbound leads converting to pipeline when prospects can book straight after the form ([Chili Piper](https://www.chilipiper.com/products/form-concierge)).

### AI coding sellers win on named deliverables and controls, not multipliers

Credible sellers of AI coding team setup sell **a working method in the client's own repository**, and list what is left behind:

- **Rasmus Widing** (Tallinn) delivers "CLAUDE.md files, PRP templates, working workflows" plus 30 days of async support ([Widing](https://rasmuswiding.com/services/workshops/)).
- **Harness Institute** sells "Harness as a Service" at a **fixed $14,000 (≈€12,000) for "One workflow installed in repository"**. It answers security with "Reviewers keep a gate on every AI-assisted change" and "MCP stays on an allowlist". It measures ROI as "review time, escaped defects, cycle time" and explicitly rejects generic "20-40% productivity" claims ([Claude Workshop](https://www.claudeworkshop.com/training)).

Credibility comes from the seller's own shipped work:

- Matt Pocock cites "1,000+ commits using Claude Code" ([AI Hero](https://www.aihero.dev/cohorts/claude-code-for-real-engineers-2026-04)).
- Ken Kousen publishes his full training materials on GitHub ([Kousen](https://github.com/kousen/claude-code-training/blob/main/README.md)).

Hype markers are "Nx faster" claims and superlatives.

Published price anchors:

- Hackages (Belgium): **€650 per person per day**, about €5,200 for one day with 8 engineers ([Hackages](https://www.hackages.com/workshops)).
- Teknologisk Institut: **DKK 11,999 (≈€1,610) per participant** for a 2-day Claude Code course, currently with "No new dates available" ([Teknologisk](https://www.teknologisk.dk/kurser/claude-code/k92023)).
- Golo Roden: a €49 public webinar ([heise](https://www.heise.de/blog/Agentic-Engineering-Webinar-zum-verlaesslichen-Arbeiten-mit-Claude-Code-11413500.html)).

In Denmark the only visible specialist is Snilld in Odense. Its page answers buyer questions well ("Er AI-genereret kode sikkert?") but shows no prices and no proof ([Snilld](https://snilld.dk/claude-code/)).

Demand is growing. AI use among Danish firms with 10+ employees rose from 15% in 2023 to **59% in 2026** ([NordiskPost on Danmarks Statistik](https://www.nordiskpost.com/2026/09/17/ai-use-nordic-companies/)). Nordic firms name skills gaps as a top barrier (39%) ([Tieto](https://www.tieto.com/en/campaigns/nordic-ai-survey-2026/)). Globally, only 31% of developers use AI agents, and trust in AI accuracy fell to about 29% ([Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)). This points to demand for guided setup, not more tools. It also points to two objections the site should answer directly: safety and measurable payoff.

The pattern is consistent. Almost every gap is either **material already written but hidden** (eyebrow, proof context), **something withheld** (price, photo, dates), or **an operational promise not yet backed** (reply time, availability).

The existing strengths are real and should be kept:

- the symptom lists ("The cloud bill grows faster than the business");
- the honest labels on each figure ("Measured in production", "Tested on two months of historical data");
- the short form with `?topic=` preselection, the same mechanism Kevin Leary uses;
- the CVR shown on every page.

The home page also already shows the three-rung ladder the best peers use.

### Larger decisions worth making deliberately

**Publishing prices** is the biggest single decision. The case for it:

- it matches what buyers say they want (81%, [TrustRadius via eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/b2b-tech-buyers-crave-pricing-transparency));
- it filters out budgets that don't fit;
- it differentiates in a market where 88% of consultants show no fees ([Consulting Success](https://www.consultingsuccess.com/consulting-statistics)) and no Danish or Nordic solo seller publishing prices was found.

The case against:

- it removes the chance to price on value (the Weiss school);
- there is no services-specific evidence that it raises enquiries.

The defensible middle ground, recommended here, is **"from €X" on the two fixed-scope products**, plus a stated minimum for hours. Hourly work can stay unpriced, or show the Danish market band as context.

Anchors for choosing X, all from public sources:

- **Danish rate guides.** Senior freelance IT at DKK 800–1,200/hour (≈€107–161), or DKK 6,000–9,000 (≈€804–1,206) per day. The specialist or architect band is DKK 1,000–1,600/hour, or DKK 7,500–12,000 (≈€1,005–1,609) per day ([LønRadar 2026](https://loenradar.dk/en/guides/freelance-it-hourly-rate-denmark-2026)). This is a guide without a disclosed method.
- **The review.** Multiplying the site's own estimated 5–8 days by the senior day-rate band gives roughly **€4,000–9,650**. Hikmah's one-week audit is ≈€2,600.
- **The AI coding setup.** 6–10 days at the same band gives roughly **€4,800–12,100**. For comparison, Harness charges ≈€12,000 fixed per workflow, and Hackages about €5,200 for one training day with 8 engineers.

These are arithmetic on sourced inputs, not recommendations of his price. His actual rates stay out of this public repository until he chooses to publish them. Pair the published price with a **risk reversal**: the review fee credited against follow-on work, which is the most common device among peers who publish prices. Mathias decides both.

**Calendar booking** is a hybrid decision: keep the form, and offer a "book a 20-minute call" link in the success state and on /contact. The vendor data favours it, but that data is weak and aimed at sales teams ([Chili Piper](https://www.chilipiper.com/products/form-concierge)). It needs a cal.com or similar account and a decision on which hours to expose.

**Content as the engine** is slower but compounding. Two data points support it:

- 81.5% of firms get referrals from people who never worked with them, driven by expertise such as blog posts and articles (20%) ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/referral-marketing-and-your-professional-services-brand)).
- Adding statistics and citations lifted visibility in generative-AI answers in controlled tests (summarised in [Frase](https://www.frase.io/blog/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo)).

The blog should be repositioned from "Data Science, AI/ML, career" to the two offers, in English first with Danish where it helps local search. It should start with first-hand, labelled write-ups: the incremental-load rewrite behind the −98.5% figure, and how the site itself is built with coding agents. LinkedIn posts should link to these rather than to the home page. Ranking for broad terms is unrealistic for a new site. Specific long-tail pages ("data platform review", "Claude Code setup for a development team", Danish equivalents) are the realistic target. Keyword volumes were not available and should be checked in a keyword tool with a Danish location.

**A self-check lead magnet** is optional and backed by the weakest evidence: peer patterns, no conversion data. Options are a data platform cost and health checklist, or an AI coding readiness checklist. It is the most common secondary CTA among stronger solos, so it is a reasonable later addition once proof and prices are in place.

**A broker-ready profile** is a downloadable one-page CV with skill keywords. It serves the Danish broker channel and the account managers who vet consultants. Public buyers above the 2026–27 service thresholds (state DKK 1,044,400 ≈ €140,000; municipal DKK 1,611,360 ≈ €216,000, [KFST](https://kfst.dk/udbud/regler-og-lovgivning/taerskelvaerdier)) mostly buy through SKI suppliers. Long public engagements will therefore come via brokers. The fixed-scope products fall well below those thresholds and can be bought directly.

**The name spread** needs a decision at some point. "mlnanalytics.com", "MLN Data Consulting" and "Mathias Lau Nielsen" are three identities for a stranger to connect, and "analytics" matches neither offer. It is a low priority next to the items above, and changing the domain has costs, but it should be a conscious choice.

## Measure from a zero baseline, because most of the evidence is borrowed

The domain went live on 2026-09-30, so there is no baseline yet. Almost all the evidence above comes from other contexts: software buyers, sales teams, landing pages in general, or peer sites without conversion data. The site already tracks `enquiry_sent` with topic and locale, which is enough to tell which offer attracts enquiries and whether a change such as publishing a price shifts volume or mix.

The practical sequence:

1. Ship the quick wins and item A first, since they are either safe or fix correctness.
2. Record enquiries per topic per month.
3. Introduce prices and case cards as distinct, dated changes, so their effect can be seen.

Unbounce's 6.1% median for professional-services landing pages ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/professional-services-conversion-rate/)) is a loose reference at best, because a consultant site with mostly referred visitors is not a paid landing page. Lead quality matters more than rate for a solo practice: count how many enquiries turn into a call, and how many calls into paid work.

## Conclusion

The site's shortfall is not design or structure. It **withholds** the things a stranger needs in order to trust a one-person firm: what it costs to start, who has benefited, what he looks like, and whether he will answer. The peers that sell best on their own sites make the first purchase small, priced and reversible, and back each offer with one concrete number in the buyer's terms. MLN already has the numbers and the ladder. It mainly needs to attach them to the right pages and commit to a price and a face.

Two local facts sharpen the opportunity. In Denmark, the visible competition for both offers is either brokers, institutes, or specialists without prices or proof, so a solo engineer with a priced review, labelled case cards and a documented AI coding setup would stand out on content alone. The AI coding offer is the weaker half on proof but has an unusual asset: the site's own repository is public and was built with the method it sells. Turning that into a deliberate, inspectable worked example is the cheapest credible proof available, and it follows the site's rule against invented numbers without needing a client's permission.
