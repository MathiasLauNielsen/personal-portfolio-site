Audit by Claude on 2026-09-30 (live site fetched about 17:17 UTC, repository at dc02a4a), after Mathias asked for "an SEO pass: a site can be built perfectly but if no one sees it, it doesn't matter."

## State found

- Technical base sound: unique titles and descriptions, self canonicals, reciprocal en/da/x-default hreflang, one link-preview image per page, organisation and FAQ structured data, `index, follow`, http and www redirect with 308 to the bare domain, sitemap with language alternates. Nothing blocked indexing.
- Defects: `robots.txt` disallowed `/api`, which includes `/api/og`, the link-preview image on every page; LinkedIn's and X's bots honour robots.txt, so previews probably rendered without an image. The sitemap stamped every URL with the build time as `lastModified`. Four descriptions were over 160 characters. `/blog` (empty) had no canonical and was indexable; blog posts had no canonical or preview; `/blog` and `/privatlivspolitik` inherited the home page's preview text. No title contained the words buyers search for ("freelance", "data engineer", "konsulent", "Copenhagen/København"). No Search Console or Bing verification anywhere.
- `site:mlnanalytics.com` returned nothing; searching "MLN Data Consulting" or the full name returned unrelated firms and people. No known backlinks. No Google Business Profile, broker or directory listings recorded.

## What the evidence says drives visibility for a new one-person consultancy

Ordered by expected yield. Most sources are vendor surveys; the direction is consistent.

1. Referrals and LinkedIn, by a wide margin.
2. Brokers in Denmark (7N, emagine, Right People Group, Worksome, findITconsultants.com); their account managers check the website.
3. Directory listings, which AI answer engines cite more than vendor sites.
4. A Google Business Profile as a service-area business with hidden address (eligible because he works on-site).
5. Bing Webmaster Tools with import from Search Console (Bing feeds ChatGPT and Copilot).
6. A link from this public repository's README and the GitHub profile.

Not worth doing: `llms.txt` or "AI optimisation" rewrites (Google states no special files or markup are needed), chasing head terms owned by Toptal/Upwork/freelancermap, Trustpilot or Yelp, paid links. In the Danish market the job title is searched in English ("data engineer"); "dataingeniør" is rare. Keyword volumes need a Google Ads account (Keyword Planner); no free source gives them.

## Honest expectation

Within 6–12 months search can deliver rankings for the name and company (needed for referral verification), correct link previews, and possibly page one for thin Danish long-tail phrases around named tools. It will not produce meaningful enquiry volume on its own for a new one-person domain. Search Console should be set up before more title changes so a baseline exists.

## Shipped from the audit (PR "ML as a named strength, and the search visibility pass")

Titles and descriptions per page in both languages with the searched words; robots.txt now disallows only `/admin` and `/api/kontakt`; sitemap without build-time dates; person structured data with URL, LinkedIn and the technologies from the offer pages; entity sentence on the About page naming BigQuery, Google Cloud, Microsoft Fabric and Claude Code; `/blog` noindex while empty, canonicals and previews for blog posts and the privacy policy; README describes the site and links to it.
